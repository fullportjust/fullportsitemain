How to Read a Trade Signal: [BUY] → [TRIM] → [SELL]

Options basics · ~6 min read

A trade signal is how an analyst shows their trade in real time — when they opened it, when they took some profit off the table, and when they closed. At Full Port University, every signal uses three tags: [BUY] to open a position, [TRIM] to sell part of it and lock in gains, and [SELL] to close what’s left. Learn those three tags and you can follow along — even on day one.

What a trade signal actually is

A signal is a short message that names a specific options contract and what the analyst did with it. You get the ticker, the expiration, the strike, whether it’s a call or a put, and the price the analyst paid. Then, as the trade plays out, follow-up tags show when they took profit and when they exited. That’s the point of being inside with live analysts: you see a full trade managed in real time, not just an entry.

The three tags, in order

[BUY] — open the position

A buy signal is the entry. It names the exact contract and the price the analyst paid. Here’s what one looks like inside Discord:

Kenny Analyst[BUY] XOM 11/4 $104 Calls @ 2.20 — liking the strength on XOM again; holding up well despite market chop.

Read it left to right: XOM is the ticker, 11/4 is the expiration date, $104 is the strike price, Calls means you profit if the stock goes up, and @ 2.20 is the entry price per contract ($220, since one contract controls 100 shares).

[TRIM] — take some profit

To trim is to sell part of your position — sometimes a quarter, sometimes more — while letting the rest run. It locks in real gains and takes the pressure off, so the plan on the rest is to walk away flat rather than hand the gain back. This is the tag that saves beginners from their own emotions.

Kenny Analyst[TRIM] XOM calls here for +40%. Sold 3/4, holding 1/4 with stops moved to breakeven.
Why trim instead of holding for more? Options move fast in both directions. Trimming banks a win you actually keep, and moving your stop up to what you paid means the plan on the rest is to walk away flat rather than hand the gain back — though stops aren't guaranteed to fill at your price in a fast market.

[SELL] — close it out

A sell signal closes what’s left of the position. The trade is done — the trim was booked earlier, and the rest is out. No signal is a “hold forever”; every trade has an exit.

Kenny Analyst[SELL] Closing the rest of XOM here. Clean trade — on to the next.

Reading a buy alert, piece by piece

  • Ticker — the stock or ETF (e.g. XOM, SPY, QQQ).
  • Expiration — the date the contract expires. Sooner = cheaper but riskier.
  • Strike — the price level the option is betting on.
  • Call or Put — a call profits when the stock rises; a put profits when it falls.
  • Entry price — what the analyst paid per contract. Multiply by 100 for the dollar cost.

Position sizing: Full, Small, Lotto

Signals often include how big the play is meant to be. Sizing is how you manage risk — not every trade deserves the same amount of money.

  • Full — a standard, higher-conviction position.
  • Small — reduced size for a riskier or more speculative setup.
  • Lotto — a tiny, high-risk / high-reward flyer. Only money you’re completely fine losing.

DTE and why it matters

DTE means Days To Expiration — how long until the contract expires. Fewer days means the option is cheaper, but time decay eats its value faster, so it’s less forgiving if the trade moves against you. More days costs more but gives the trade room to work. As a beginner, leaning toward more DTE is usually the safer way to learn.

Putting it together

One full trade, start to finish: you get the [BUY] and open XOM $104 calls at $2.20. A day later the analyst posts [TRIM] at +40% — you sell most of your contracts and bank the profit, moving your stop to breakeven on the rest. Then a [SELL] closes the remainder. You watched a complete trade instead of a tip — entry, trim, exit — and picked up the why at each step. That's a normal day inside.

FAQ

What does [TRIM] mean in options trading? +
To trim is to sell part of your position — sometimes a quarter, sometimes more — to lock in profit while letting the rest of the trade keep running. It reduces risk without fully exiting.
What does DTE mean? +
DTE stands for Days To Expiration — how many days are left until an options contract expires. Shorter DTE is cheaper but riskier because time decay works against you faster.
How much of my account should one trade be? +
Never risk your whole account on a single trade. We generally suggest around $500 to trade comfortably, and use position sizes (Full, Small, Lotto) so higher-risk plays stay small.
Do I have to take every signal? +
No. Signals are educational — they show what our analysts are doing so you can learn from it. You decide which trades fit your risk tolerance, and you place your own trades.
